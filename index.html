Our craft

“We don’t chase seasons. We chase the perfect plain weave.”

Every Tana garment begins on a pit loom in Kerala or a rain-fed flax field in Flanders. We buy whole cloth, not trend forecasts — then cut it into the few shapes we believe in: a tee that holds its neckline, a trouser that pleats once and falls clean, a jacket that softens with every year.

Run sizes stay between 120 and 400 pieces. When a cloth is gone, we wait for the next harvest.

The cloth library

Four fibres, nothing hidden

Every label lists the mill, the weave and the dye. If we can’t trace it, we don’t cut it.

Khadi cotton

Handspun · handwoven · Kerala

Slubby, breathable, stronger when washed. Our oldest cloth and still the one we reach for first.

Belgian linen

Rain-fed flax · Flanders

Grown without irrigation, retted in the field, woven loose enough to move air. Creases are part of the deal.

Merino jersey

RWS certified · non-mulesed

Fine-gauge, 18.5 micron, knitted in Portugal. Warm without bulk, and it genuinely resists odour.

Peace silk noil

Ahimsa · Karnataka

Spun from the short fibres left after reeling, so nothing is wasted. Dry, matte and softly irregular.
